# UI Design Bible: Retro Arcade Workshop

Single source of truth for the Road Builder Racer UI. Do not change an approved direction without asking.
Status: Home approved (pegboard redrawn). Garage, Worlds, HUD, Result, Settings and Collection rebuilt in the same look; awaiting review.

## 1. Theme metaphor
A retro arcade racing garage: pegboard tools, hanging lamps, a roll-up garage door, license plates, steel plates, race
tickets and masking tape. The player stands inside the workshop and looks out through the garage door at the live game
world, where the equipped car waits. Reference for usability and playful personality only: Hill Climb Racing 2 (no art copied).

## 2. Shape language
Primary: riveted steel plates with notched corners, hexagonal badges, license plates. Secondary: masking tape, ticket paper,
stickers, ribbons. Decorations are rotated 2 to 12 degrees, overlap the plates and are never tappable.
Every major element has a designed silhouette (notched corners, offset backing, rivets). No plain rectangles.

## 4. Typography and hierarchy
TextMeshPro only. Lilita One for titles, buttons and numbers. Baloo 2 for body and captions. Hierarchy: title plate
(68), main action (56), secondary actions (44), numbers (46 to 52), captions (24).

## 5. Iconography
Illustrated, 3 px ink outline, flat colour with one highlight and one shade. Coin medallion stands proud of its plate. Gear is
engraved into a hex badge. Pixel accents only inside the backdrop.

## 6. Texture and material language
Brushed-steel bevels (light top band, dark bottom band), rivets with a highlight, hazard stripes, paper with torn or
perforated edges, tape at 85% opacity. The backdrop is real pixel art at 384x216, shown at 5x with Point filter, with a
transparent door opening so the live world shows through.

## 7. Motion personality
Weighty and mechanical: the title plate drops on its chains (0.45 s, OutBack), controls pop in one after another (0.35 s,
OutBack, 70 ms stagger), buttons squash on press and spring back. Nothing loops. All tweens are killed on hide.

## 8. Sound personality
Mechanical clicks, small metallic impacts, paper and stamp sounds, short arcade confirmations. No UI sound files are
licensed yet; the press hook is `PressFeedback.Pressed`.

## 9. Five DO rules
1. Give every panel a silhouette and two material treatments.
2. Let tape, stickers and badges overlap plate edges.
3. Show selection as a physical change (lift, stamp, lit), not only colour.
4. Put the live game world in the window of the screen where it makes sense.
5. Draw the art first, then build the screen from it.

## 10. Five DON'T rules
1. No plain or grey rounded rectangles.
2. No identical card grids.
3. No glossy web buttons or Material shadows.
4. No hardcoded colours, fonts or sprite paths.
5. No game logic in views.

## Hero direction
HOME is the hero screen: title plate on chains, coin counter plate and settings badge at the top corners, best-distance
license plate on the right, big orange arcade START RACE button flanked by two steel tabs, garage door window onto the world.

## Asset rendering style
Hybrid: smooth illustrated plates (drawn at 3x and downsampled, Bilinear) and a pixel-art backdrop (Point). Generated by
`tools/ui/workshop_home.py`; everything is procedural and reproducible. Ledger: `Docs/UI/UI_Asset_Ledger.csv` (status
GENERATED_REVIEW until the owner approves) and `Docs/ASSET_LEDGER.md`.

## Responsive principles (updated)
The garage background is separate parts, not one picture: wall, floor, hazard line and ceiling beam are tiles that repeat sideways;
the door is one centred sprite with a see-through opening; props hug the left or right screen edge; ink and floor-coloured fills cover
screens taller than 1080. So any aspect ratio (4:3, 16:9, 21:9) fills without bars. Verified at 1600x1200, 1920x1080 and 2400x1080.
All screens are placed in `Main.unity` under UIRoot/SafeArea (menu Road Builder > UI > Place Screens In Main Scene); `ScreenStack` uses
those instances and does not spawn them at run time. After rebuilding prefabs (Build Screen Prefabs) the instances follow automatically.

## Responsive principles (original)
Reference 1920x1080, CanvasScaler Expand. The backdrop is a fixed 1920x1080 centred image; four ink bars cover wider or
taller screens. Controls sit inside the safe area. Verified at 1920x1080 (and 1280x720, which scales to the same) and 2400x1080.

## Stand-alone props (animatable parts)
Nothing in the garage is baked into the backdrop. The backdrop (`UI_Background_Workshop_Home` with a door opening,
`UI_Background_Workshop_Wall` without) is only wall, floor, beam and door frame. Every object is its own sprite in
`Assets/Art/UI/Workshop/Props/` (pegboard, wrench, hammer, screwdriver, saw, gear, toolbox, two lamps and their glows, clock
face and two hands, robot body, eyes, antenna and two arms, shelf, three cans, two tyres, oil drum), listed in
`WorkshopPropSet` (read by id) and placed per screen by `UIPrefabBuilder.Props` (Full on Home, Light on Garage, Lamps elsewhere).
`WorkshopProps` animates them by child name: lamps sway, the clock ticks, the robot blinks and its antenna pulses; tapping a tool,
the robot, a can or the drum plays a stunt (hammer swing, gear spin, cans topple, robot cheers). To add a stunt, add a part to
`tools/ui/workshop_props.py`, a row to `PropTable`, and a case to `WorkshopProps`.
